This page visualises the closed knight's tour produced by BJMOW Algorithm 1 (Besa et al., 2022), optionally patched: five adjacent $4\times8$ Sequence1 heel templates are replaced, in groups of five, by a single $4\times40$ SequenceP_40 template found by CP-SAT search.

Toggle the checkbox to switch between the two tours. Edges that differ between BJMOW's tour and the patched tour are drawn in colour: red when viewing the patched tour (these are the new edges contributed by SequenceP_40) and green when viewing the BJMOW tour (these are the Sequence1 edges that the patch replaces). All other edges (the vast majority, drawn in dark blue) are bit-for-bit identical between the two tours. Try $n = 80, 120, 160$ to see the difference region grow.

About

The knight's tour problem asks for a cycle through every cell of a rectangular board using only knight moves. BJMOW (Theoretical Computer Science 902, 2022; arXiv:1904.02824) gave the first $O(1)$-constant upper bound on the number of crossings: $\chi(T_n) \le 12n + O(1)$ asymptotically.

This fork adds a single-element SequenceP_40 patch in board.js: a $4 \times 40$ pairing-preserving template found by CP-SAT search. It replaces five adjacent $4 \times 8$ Sequence1 heel templates and preserves the cross-boundary edge interface; BJMOW's Hamilton-property proof (their Theorem 2) therefore applies verbatim to the patched tour. The crossing constant drops asymptotically from $12$ to $11.5$.

Original demo by Juan Jose Besa Vidal, Timothy Johnson, Nil Mamano, and Martha Osegueda (upstream repo). Source for this fork: github.com/daizisheng/MinCrossingsKnightsTour.
